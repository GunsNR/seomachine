# Competitive scorecard

Governed by `docs/product-constitution.md` §2.

**Every scorecard cell in this document is currently unmeasured.** That is the
honest state and it is stated up front rather than filled with plausible
estimates. A scorecard populated from impressions would be exactly the kind of
fabricated benchmark Gate 0 removed from the product.

---

## 1. The objective this scorecard serves

> Rank Logic must maintain a measured competitive advantage for its target
> customer and priority workflows. Every material claim of superiority must be
> backed by a dated, reproducible benchmark, real-user evidence, or verified
> product data.

Not "we are better than Semrush". That claim is unfalsifiable and therefore
worthless. The enforceable version names a customer, a workflow, a measurement
and a date.

---

## 2. Evidence rules

A cell may be filled only by one of:

| Evidence type | What it requires |
| --- | --- |
| **Dated reproducible benchmark** | A written method, a date, inputs anyone can re-run, and the raw result retained |
| **Real-user evidence** | Observed task performance with representative users, n stated, method stated |
| **Verified product data** | A capability the registry marks `verified` or `beta`, with its evidence pointer resolving |
| **Published vendor fact** | A competitor's own current public documentation, cited by URL and retrieval date |

Never acceptable: a marketing page read as a feature inventory; a competitor's
claim taken at face value; recollection; an analyst summary; a comparison
generated by an AI without a primary source.

A cell with no evidence stays empty. An empty cell is information — it says
nobody has measured this yet.

Any public comparison page is generated only from filled cells, and every filled
cell carries its evidence and date.

## 4. What gets benchmarked

Customer outcomes, not feature counts. A longer feature list is not a competitive
advantage — Section 2 of the constitution is explicit that success is the
customer completing work more accurately, quickly and confidently.

| # | Dimension | Measurement method | Rank Logic today |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Setup time and time to first useful insight | Timed task, new account to first insight the user calls useful | Unmeasured |
| 2 | Coverage, freshness, accuracy and provenance of data | Sampled comparison against a known-good reference | Unmeasured. Provenance is implemented and tested; coverage is currently zero for AI visibility |
| 3 | Ability to identify the correct next action | Task test — user names the right action from the interface | Unmeasured. No Action Center exists (Phase 5) |
| 4 | Task completion rate and time | Moderated usability test, five priority workflows | Unmeasured |
| 5 | Recommendation → approval → publish conversion | Product analytics once the chain exists | Unmeasured. Chain incomplete (Phase 6) |
| 6 | Successful WordPress publishing and rollback | End-to-end run against a real installation | Unmeasured. Stub-tested only (Phase 3) |
| 7 | Reporting clarity for non-experts | Comprehension test with business-owner participants | Unmeasured. No reporting engine (Phase 7) |
| 8 | Accessibility and mobile usability | Automated checks plus manual keyboard pass; mobile task test | Unmeasured |
| 9 | Measurable customer outcomes | Pilot programme with permission | Unmeasured (Phase 8) |
| 10 | Total cost to the customer | Published pricing plus provider pass-through | Unmeasured |
| 11 | Trust, security and operational reliability | Security review, incident record, uptime | Partially known — open items in the truth audit |

## 5. Where an advantage is plausible, and what would prove it

Stated as hypotheses. None is a claim.

| Hypothesis | Why it might hold | What would prove it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Provenance and evidence quality | Gate 0 and Gate 1 built run identity, coverage, confidence intervals and insufficient-evidence handling into the data model rather than the presentation layer. That is unusual and hard to retrofit. | A side-by-side task where users are asked how much to trust a number, scored on whether they can find its source |
| Combined Google and AI visibility in one workflow | Most products treat AI visibility as a separate module. The target IA puts them on one screen. | Task test — time to answer "where am I losing ground overall" |
| WordPress and Elementor execution depth | A real plugin, REST integration, block publishing and an Elementor kit already exist. | Phase 3 verification against a real install, then a timed publish-and-rollback comparison |
| Agency and client simplicity | Agency-first wedge, real client work as the design input | Pilot evidence (Phase 8) |
| Recommendation-to-outcome learning | Outcome learning is designed in from Phase 0 rather than bolted on | Requires Phases 6 and 8 |

Note the honest reading of this table: **four of the five hypotheses cannot be
tested until later phases complete.** Only the first is testable soon, and only
after Phase 1 gives it real data to be provenance-bearing about.

## 6. Where the product is behind, and why that is expected

- **Classic SEO data breadth.** No SERP, rank or backlink provider is connected.
  Semrush and Ahrefs have a decade of index investment. Parity is bought through
  licensing (Phase 4), not built.
- **Local SEO.** Nothing exists.
- **Reporting.** Nothing exists.
- **AI visibility.** The pipeline is complete and the sources are absent — the
  one place where the product is behind its own marketing thesis, which is why
  Phase 1 is sequenced first.

Reaching reliable parity on table-stakes workflows comes before winning
decisively anywhere. A weak clone of every competitor feature is explicitly not
the goal.

---

## 7. Cadence

- Re-audit the named set quarterly, or immediately on a major release by a
  competitor in the AI-visibility space.
- Re-run any filled benchmark before citing it publicly if it is older than six
  months. A stale benchmark presented as current is the same error as a stale
  metric presented as fresh.
- Record each audit as a dated entry with its sources.

---

## 8. Legal and ethical boundary

Never copy competitor source code, protected assets, testimonials, copy or
distinctive creative expression. Workflows and interaction principles may be
learned from; the expression must be original.

Comparison pages state facts with citations and dates, describe competitors
accurately including their strengths, and never imply endorsement.
